    thumbv7em-none-eabihf riscv32imac-unknown-none-elf
  RUSTFLAGS='-Z emit-stack-sizes' \
    cargo +nightly-2026-08-21 build \
      --manifest-path crates/qwp-e-size/Cargo.toml --release --target <target>

Measured frames (bytes):

| Function | thumbv7em | riscv32imac |
|---|---:|---:|
| `qwp_e::verifier::wots_verify` | 6,048 | 5,968 |
| `qwp_hash::blake3::hash32_with_key` | 5,808 | 5,808 |
| `qwp_e::verifier::verify_common` | 264 | 256 |
| `qwp_hash::blake3::Hasher::combine_root` | 296 | 192 |
| `qwp_hash::blake3::Hasher::push_chunk_cv` | 240 | 192 |
| `qwp_hash::blake3::compress` | 216 | 160 |
| `qwp_hash::blake3::Hasher::finalize_chunk` | 152 | 144 |

Interpretation:
- The peak verifier frame is about **6.0 KB**, driven by the BLAKE3 hasher
  (`chunk_buf[1024]` plus the 54-level `stack`). It is fixed-size and
  allocation-free, but it is much larger than the 67-byte digit buffer.
- The `qwp-e-size` probe functions additionally place the caller-owned
  signature/key buffers on the stack (1.8-2.5 KB); a real caller that keeps the
  signature in RAM can avoid that copy.
- A deployment on a small MCU must budget roughly 6 KB of stack for the
  verifier plus the caller's key/signature storage. This is a documented
  constraint, not a claim that QHS-E fits an 8 KB device.

No stack overflow was observed in host tests; the numbers are static frame
sizes, not a runtime high-water mark.
